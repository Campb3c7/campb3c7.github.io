1 SA node Right atrium, near SVC 60–100 Primary pacemaker — sets normal HR
2 Atria depolarize RA → LA — P wave on ECG; atrial contraction
3 AV node Floor of RA, near septum 40–60 Delays ~0.1 s — lets atria finish filling ventricles
4 Bundle of His Top of interventricular septum — Carries signal into ventricles
5 L & R bundle branches Down each side of septum — Splits signal to each ventricle simultaneously
6 Purkinje fibers Ventricular walls 20–40 Rapid spread → simultaneous contraction (QRS)
Rate hierarchy: The lower the structure, the slower its backup rate. SA fails → AV takes over (40–60). Both fail → Purkinje escape (20–40). Higher pacemakers always "outrun" lower ones — this is why SA node normally controls everything.

Heart Sounds

SoundWhat closesPhase
S1 — "lub" AV valves (tricuspid + mitral) Start of systole (ventricular contraction begins)
S2 — "dub" Semilunar valves (pulmonic + aortic) Start of diastole (ventricular relaxation begins)
Rule: At any moment, exactly one set of valves is open. AV open → semilunar closed (filling). Semilunar open → AV closed (ejecting). They never open simultaneously.

Chordae Tendineae & Papillary Muscles

The "heartstrings" — anchor AV valve cusps to the ventricular wall. They prevent prolapse (backflow) when the ventricle contracts forcefully. Semilunar valves don't need them — their cusp geometry and arterial backpressure snap them shut automatically.

Vessel Sequence

🧠 "All Arteries Are Calm, Very Very Vascular"
Aorta → Arteries → Arterioles → Capillaries → Venules → Veins → Vena cava
Pressure drops the whole way. Velocity is slowest at capillaries — where exchange happens.
VesselWallPressureFunction
Aorta / Large arteries Thick, elastic High & pulsatile "Windkessel" — stretches in systole, recoils in diastole to smooth flow
Arterioles Thick smooth muscle, narrow lumen Sharp drop Resistance vessels — primary site of BP regulation
Capillaries Single endothelial cell layer Low Exchange vessels — O₂, CO₂, nutrients, waste

BP Equation — Tying It Together

BP = CO × SVR
CO = HR × SV  |  SVR set by arterioles

CO (cardiac output) is controlled by the heart — rate (HR) and volume per beat (SV).
SVR (systemic vascular resistance) is controlled by the arterioles.

To raise BP, the body has exactly two levers: pump more (↑CO) or squeeze the arterioles (↑SVR).
