The Big Picture

One system accelerates, one brakes

🧠 "Fight or Flight vs. Rest and Digest"
Sympathetic = fight or flight — more blood, faster, harder, wider pipes to muscle.
Parasympathetic = rest & digest — slow the heart, conserve energy. Minimal effect on vessels.

⚡ Sympathetic Pathway

→Origin: thoracolumbar spinal cord (T1–L2)
→Preganglionic fiber → sympathetic ganglion
→Postganglionic releases norepinephrine (NE)
→Adrenal medulla also releases epinephrine (Epi) into blood
→Receptors: β1 (heart), α1 (vessels), β2 (some vessels)

🌿 Parasympathetic Pathway

→Origin: brainstem + sacral cord (craniosacral)
→Vagus nerve (CN X) → cardiac ganglia
→Postganglionic releases acetylcholine (ACh)
→Receptors: M2 muscarinic (heart)
→No significant innervation of most blood vessels

Memory trick — the four -tropies:
Chrono = rate  |  Ino = force  |  Dromo = conduction speed  |  Lusi = relaxation
Sympathetic increases all four. Parasympathetic only meaningfully opposes the first three, and mostly at the SA and AV nodes.

Mechanism — How Sympathetic Increases Force (β1 → cAMP)

This is the chain you need to know — every step has a testable consequence.

StepEventResult

Receptors — What's Where & What They Do

Receptor Quick Reference

🧠 "β1 = 1 heart, β2 = 2 lungs, α1 = squeeze vessels"
β1 is the dominant cardiac receptor. α1 is the dominant vascular receptor. β2 dilates vessels in muscle.
ReceptorSystemLocationLigandEffect
β1 Sympathetic SA node, AV node, ventricular myocytes NE > Epi ↑ HR, ↑ force, ↑ conduction, ↑ relaxation speed
α1 Sympathetic Vascular smooth muscle (most vessels) NE > Epi Vasoconstriction — ↑ SVR, ↑ BP
β2 Sympathetic Skeletal muscle vessels, coronary, bronchi Epi > NE Vasodilation — routes blood to working muscle
α2 Sympathetic Presynaptic terminals; some vessels NE Presynaptic: inhibits further NE release (feedback). Vascular: mild constriction
M2 Parasympathetic SA node, AV node, atrial myocytes ACh ↓ HR, ↓ conduction, ↓ atrial contractility
Why NE vs Epi matters: NE (released from nerve terminals) hits α1 and β1 strongly. Epi (released from adrenal medulla into blood) hits β2 strongly at low doses — causing vasodilation in muscle — and α1 at high doses. This is why IV epinephrine can either raise or lower SVR depending on dose.

Effects on Vascular Structures

Sympathetic — Regional Vascular Effects

The sympathetic system doesn't affect all vessels the same way — it redirects blood where it's needed during stress.

Vessel BedReceptorEffectPurpose
Skin, gut, kidneys α1 Vasoconstriction Redirect blood away from non-essential organs
Skeletal muscle β2 (Epi) / α1 (NE) Vasodilation (Epi) or mild constriction (NE) Supply working muscle during exercise
Coronary arteries β2 + metabolic Net vasodilation Feed harder-working myocardium
Veins (capacitance) α1 Venoconstriction Shifts blood from venous reservoir → ↑ preload → ↑ SV (Frank-Starling)
Arterioles (overall) α1 dominant ↑ SVR ↑ BP = ↑ CO × ↑ SVR
Net effect during fight-or-flight: ↑ HR + ↑ contractility + ↑ preload (venoconstriction) + ↑ SVR = massive ↑ in BP and cardiac output. Blood shifts from gut/skin → heart/muscle/brain.

Parasympathetic — Vascular Effects

🌿 Minimal direct effect on most vessels

Most blood vessels have no parasympathetic innervation. The PSNS controls the heart strongly but doesn't directly control vascular tone in the way the SNS does. When HR drops via vagal tone, BP falls — but that's a cardiac effect, not a vascular one.

Exception — vasodilation via ACh: Some vessels (especially in erectile tissue, salivary glands, and coronary arteries) receive parasympathetic input. ACh causes endothelial cells to release NO (nitric oxide) → vascular smooth muscle relaxation → vasodilation.
LocationEffectMechanism
Most systemic vesselsNo direct effectNo innervation
Coronary arteriesMild vasodilationACh → endothelial NO release
Erectile tissue / glandsVasodilationACh → NO → smooth muscle relaxation

Clinical pearl: Atropine (M2 blocker) → removes vagal brake → ↑ HR. Beta-blockers (β1 blocker) → ↓ HR, ↓ force, ↓ BP. Alpha-blockers (α1 blocker) → vasodilation, ↓ SVR, ↓ BP. Knowing the receptor tells you the drug effect.
